Real-Time Nonlinear MPC for a Nano-Quadrotor

[Project · code on GitHub]

TL;DR

A nonlinear model predictive controller for a nano-quadrotor. Fifty times a second it re-plans the next half second of flight with the full nonlinear dynamics and each rotor’s real thrust limits, in 0.6–0.8 ms on average. In simulation it tracks a fast figure-eight to within a millimetre, and a disturbance observer cuts its error in gusty wind by a factor of 2.9.

The controller

The quadrotor is a Crazyflie 2.x, which weighs 27 g. The controller commands its total thrust \(T\) and three body torques \(\tau\), and a mixer turns these into the four rotor thrusts. The model is the full rigid-body dynamics, with the attitude as a quaternion and no small-angle approximations: position \(p\), velocity \(v\), attitude \(q\) and body rates \(\omega\), 13 numbers in all.

$$\begin{aligned} \dot p &= v, & \dot v &= \frac{T}{m}\,R(q)\,e_z - g\,e_z + \hat d,\\ \dot q &= \tfrac12\, q \otimes \begin{bmatrix}0\\ \omega\end{bmatrix}, & \dot\omega &= J^{-1}\left(\tau - \omega \times J\omega\right) \end{aligned}$$

Every 20 ms it solves an optimal control problem over the next 0.5 s, in 25 steps, starting from the measured state:

$$\begin{aligned} \min_{x,u}\quad & \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \lVert x_k - x^{\mathrm{ref}}_k\rVert_Q^2 + \lVert u_k - u^{\mathrm{ref}}_k\rVert_R^2 + \lVert x_N - x^{\mathrm{ref}}_N\rVert_P^2 \\ \text{s.t.}\quad & x_{k+1} = F_{\mathrm{RK4}}(x_k, u_k;\ \hat d), \qquad x_0 = x_{\mathrm{measured}} \\ & 0 \le M^{-1} u_k \le f_{\max} \quad \text{(each rotor's thrust)} \\ & \lvert \omega_k \rvert \le \omega_{\max}\ \text{(soft)}, \qquad \lvert u_0 - u_{\mathrm{prev}} \rvert \le \dot u_{\max}\,\Delta t \end{aligned}$$

acados generates C code for this problem and runs one real-time iteration of sequential quadratic programming (SQP-RTI) per control step, with HPIPM solving the quadratic program inside it.

The references come from differential flatness. A quadrotor can only accelerate sideways by tilting, so asking it to follow a curve while staying level contradicts itself. The acceleration the path needs fixes the direction of the thrust; with the yaw, that fixes the reference attitude, body rates and thrust.

RMSE is the root-mean-square distance from the reference and worst the largest; solve is the mean time per solve. The figure-eight is 3 m by 1.2 m, the circle is flown three times, and the take-off starts on the ground. Over the five runs, about 4,500 solves, the slowest took 4.2 ms, well inside the 20 ms control period, and the solver never reported a failure. Times are wall-clock, single-threaded, on a shared 4-vCPU cloud machine.

Holding position in gusty wind

The wind has a steady 0.8 m/s, slow swings, sudden gusts and turbulence, peaking at 1.7 m/s. Its drag is a force the controller’s model leaves out, so on its own the controller lets the quadrotor drift downwind before it reacts. A disturbance observer estimates that force, \(\hat d\), from the gap between the measured and the predicted acceleration. The controller plans with the estimate, and the references tilt to lean into it. In the same wind, the error falls from 9.2 to 3.2 mm RMSE, and the worst deviation from 25.3 to 11.9 mm.

Limits

Everything here is simulation. The simulated quadrotor obeys the same rigid-body model the controller plans with, with perfect state measurements and no motor lag, delays or sensor noise. That is why the error in calm air is below a millimetre; the wind test is the real check of robustness. Adding motor dynamics, a state estimator and delays is the next step before running it on hardware.

Built with acados, CasADi and PyBullet. The code is on GitHub under the MIT licence, with unit tests, a CI job that builds acados and flies the quadrotor, and one script that regenerates every figure and video.
