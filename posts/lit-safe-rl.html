Safe RL for Contact: Constraints, Filters and Games

[Literature Review]

Part of a series on robot learning, read against motor neuroscience: variable impedance · adaptation · passivity · safe RL · stiffness geometry · games. The overview is From Sensorimotor Neuroscience to Robot Learning.

Safe RL splits into three families: constrain the optimisation (constrained MDPs and Lagrangians), compute the set of states from which safety can be kept (reachability), or filter actions at run time. Two computed examples show the central mechanics. An integral-only Lagrange multiplier makes the cost oscillate around its limit; a PID multiplier damps it. And the set a robot can stay in is smaller than the set it is allowed to be in. For contact the hard part is that the constraint, a force or an energy, depends on an environment stiffness the robot cannot see until it touches.

1. Three formulations

Constrained MDP (Altman 1999): maximise return subject to an expected cost budget,

$$\max_\pi\ J_R(\pi) \quad \text{s.t.}\quad J_C(\pi) \le d, \qquad\text{usually solved as}\qquad \min_{\lambda\ge0}\max_\pi\ J_R(\pi) - \lambda\big(J_C(\pi) - d\big).$$

Reachability: define a margin \(\ell(s)\), positive where the state is acceptable. The safety value \(V(s) = \max_\pi \min_t \ell(s_t)\) is the worst margin the best policy can guarantee. It satisfies

$$V(s) = \min\Big\{\ell(s),\ \max_a V\big(f(s,a)\big)\Big\},$$

whose zero superlevel set is the largest set from which the constraint can be kept forever (Mitchell, Bayen and Tomlin 2005; Fisac et al. 2019 give the discounted form that RL can learn). Filters: let a task policy propose an action and override it only when necessary, either by switching to the safety policy when \(V\) gets close to zero or by solving a small QP each step. With a control barrier function \(h\) that QP is

$$u^\ast = \arg\min_u \lVert u - u_{\text{nom}}\rVert^2 \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \nabla h(x)^\top\big(f(x) + g(x)u\big) \ge -\alpha\big(h(x)\big)$$

(Ames et al. 2017).

2. Lagrangians oscillate

The multiplier update \(\lambda \leftarrow \max(0, \lambda + \eta(J_C - d))\) is an integral controller acting on the constraint violation, and the policy it steers responds with lag, through batches, momentum and value estimates. An integrator around a lagging plant oscillates. Stooke, Achiam and Abbeel (ICML 2020) made the analogy literal and added proportional and derivative terms. Figure 1 reproduces the effect on a one-parameter stand-in for a policy (momentum gradient ascent on \(\theta - \lambda\theta^2\), noisy cost, limit \(\theta^2 \le 1\)). With the integral term alone the smoothed cost peaks at 3.3 times the limit and keeps overshooting. With PID it peaks at 1.3 times during the first transient and then settles: after the first 300 updates the smoothed cost exceeds the limit by 10% in 0% of updates, against 41% for the integral-only multiplier.

Two refinements matter for robots. Threshold-conditioned policies (CCPO, NeurIPS 2023) learn one policy for a range of budgets, so a force cap can change without retraining. Müller et al. (ICML 2024) give a primal–dual method without "error cancellation": early violations cannot be paid back by later slack, which formalises why safe during training and safe at the end are different claims.

3. The set you can stay in

A constraint on position is not a safe set if the robot is moving. For a double integrator with \(|u| \le u_{\max}\) and the constraint \(|x| \le 1\), a state is recoverable only if the robot can stop before the wall: \(x + v|v|/2u_{\max} \le 1\) and the mirror condition on the other side. Figure 2 computes the safety value by iterating the Bellman equation above on a grid and recovers this boundary. In the plotted window the safe set covers 51% of the constraint set. An aggressive nominal policy aiming at \(x = 0.95\) reaches \(x = 2.25\). The same policy behind a least-restrictive filter, which overrides it only when its next state would leave the safe set, peaks at \(x = 0.99\) and settles, with the filter acting on 69 of 600 steps.

Reachability-constrained RL (Yu et al., ICML 2022) learns this largest feasible set instead of a conservative estimate. Nakamura, Peters and Bajcsy (RSS 2025) move the computation into the latent space of a world model, so the constraint can be a learned classifier on images: "the bag spilled", "the block toppled" (Figures 3 and 4). That extends safety to failures that are hard to write as a function of state, which is most contact failures.

4. Filters, and where to put them

ATACOM (Liu et al.) makes the safe action space the tangent space of the constraint manifold, so any model-free learner can act on it, demonstrated on a real air-hockey robot; Günster et al. (CoRL 2024) learn the constraint itself. Where the filter sits changes the learning problem. Oh et al. (2025) prove that RL inside a safety filter stays safe and keeps standard convergence guarantees. Markgraf et al. (2025) analyse "action aliasing": many unsafe actions map to one safe action, which degrades the policy gradient. CBF-RL (ICRA 2026) applies the barrier only during training, so the deployed policy needs no runtime filter.

5. Safety as a game

If the disturbance is unknown, assume the worst one. ISAACS (Hsu et al., L4DC 2023) co-trains a safety policy and an adversary for reach-avoid problems. Gameplay filters (Nguyen et al., CoRL 2024) use that pair at run time. Before committing to the task policy's next actions, they imagine the task policy acting for a short horizon and then the safety policy playing against the adversary for the rest; if the imagined game is lost, they fall back (Figure 5). This was demonstrated zero-shot on 36-dimensional quadrupeds under tugging. See Adversaries and Games.

6. The sensorimotor connection

  • A learned safety margin. In a precision grip, grip force exceeds the force at which the object would slip by a margin, and the margin is set from the friction sensed at contact and from memory of the object (Johansson and Westling 1984). That is a barrier with an adaptive margin, and it is learned. Robot filters usually fix the margin by hand.
  • Fast loops as filters. The nervous system stacks feedback loops of increasing latency. The long-latency stretch response, at 50–100 ms, has a gain that depends on the task and that optimal feedback control predicts (Pruszynski and Scott 2012). Reflexes are not a fixed override; their gains are set by the task above them. That suggests safety filters whose parameters a slower policy modulates.
  • Minimum intervention. Optimal feedback control corrects only deviations that matter for the goal (Todorov and Jordan 2002; review). A least-restrictive filter is the safety version: change the action only when the safe set requires it.
  • Buying margin with stiffness. When perturbations are unpredictable and destabilising, people raise impedance (Burdet et al. 2001), trading effort for a margin against disturbances they cannot model.

7. What this leaves open for contact

  • Force and energy constraints depend on unseen environment stiffness, and most filters assume a model of it. Latent safety sets learned from contact data are the obvious next step.
  • A filter matters only where its constraint activates, which is easy to miss in aggregate metrics: report how often the constraint actually binds.
