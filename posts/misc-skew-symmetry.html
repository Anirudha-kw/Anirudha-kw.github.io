Why Coriolis Forces Do No Work

[Misc]

In the dynamics of a robot arm, the Coriolis and centrifugal terms look like forces, but in total they do no work. The precise statement is that \(\dot M - 2C\) is skew-symmetric. This note derives the equations of motion from the Lagrangian, proves the property, shows on a simulated arm what it does and does not mean, and follows it to the stability proofs and adaptive controllers built on it.

1. Equations of motion from energy

For a rigid arm with joint angles \(q\), the kinetic energy is a quadratic form in the joint velocities with a configuration-dependent inertia matrix, \(T = \tfrac12\dot q^\top M(q)\dot q\), and gravity contributes a potential \(P(q)\). The Euler–Lagrange equations for \(L = T - P\),

$$\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q_k} - \frac{\partial L}{\partial q_k} = \tau_k ,$$

give, after differentiating,

$$\sum_j M_{kj}\ddot q_j + \sum_{i,j}\Big(\frac{\partial M_{kj}}{\partial q_i} - \frac12\frac{\partial M_{ij}}{\partial q_k}\Big)\dot q_i\dot q_j + \frac{\partial P}{\partial q_k} = \tau_k .$$

The middle sum is quadratic in velocity. Symmetrising it over \(i\) and \(j\) gives the Christoffel symbols of the first kind,

$$c_{ijk} = \tfrac12\Big(\frac{\partial M_{kj}}{\partial q_i} + \frac{\partial M_{ki}}{\partial q_j} - \frac{\partial M_{ij}}{\partial q_k}\Big), \qquad C_{kj} = \sum_i c_{ijk}\,\dot q_i ,$$

and the standard form \(M(q)\ddot q + C(q,\dot q)\dot q + g(q) = \tau\). The vector \(C\dot q\) is unique; the matrix \(C\) is not, since any matrix \(N\) with \(N\dot q = 0\) can be added to it. The Christoffel choice is the one with the property below.

2. The skew-symmetry, proved

Since \(\dot M_{kj} = \sum_i \frac{\partial M_{kj}}{\partial q_i}\dot q_i\), the first term of \(2c_{ijk}\) cancels against \(\dot M\):

$$(\dot M - 2C)_{kj} = \sum_i\Big(\frac{\partial M_{ij}}{\partial q_k} - \frac{\partial M_{ki}}{\partial q_j}\Big)\dot q_i .$$

Exchanging \(k\) and \(j\) and using \(M = M^\top\) turns each term into its negative, so \((\dot M - 2C)^\top = -(\dot M - 2C)\). For any skew-symmetric \(S\), \(v^\top S v = 0\); in particular

$$\dot q^\top(\dot M - 2C)\dot q = 0 .$$

This last identity holds for every valid \(C\), because \(\dot q^\top C\dot q\) depends only on the unique vector \(C\dot q\).

3. What "no work" means

Differentiate the kinetic energy and substitute the dynamics:

$$\dot T = \dot q^\top M\ddot q + \tfrac12\dot q^\top\dot M\dot q = \dot q^\top(\tau - g) + \tfrac12\,\dot q^\top(\dot M - 2C)\,\dot q = \dot q^\top(\tau - g).$$

The rate of change of kinetic energy is the power of the applied and gravitational torques alone. The velocity-dependent terms are not individually powerless: \(\dot q^\top C\dot q = \tfrac12\dot q^\top\dot M\dot q\), which is generally nonzero. What vanishes is their total together with the change in inertia, so they move energy between joints and links without creating or destroying any. Figure 2 shows this on a two-link arm swinging freely with no gravity, torque or friction: link 1's share of the kinetic energy swings between 2% and 28%, the per-joint Coriolis powers are large and opposite, and the total energy stays constant to a relative error of 1e-12 over six seconds of RK4 integration. The residual \(\dot q^\top(\dot M - 2C)\dot q\) along the trajectory never exceeds 4e-16.

For this arm the inertia and Coriolis matrices are explicit. With \(a_1 = I_1 + I_2 + m_1\ell_{c1}^2 + m_2(\ell_1^2 + \ell_{c2}^2)\), \(a_2 = m_2\ell_1\ell_{c2}\), \(a_3 = I_2 + m_2\ell_{c2}^2\) and \(h = a_2\sin q_2\),

$$M = \begin{pmatrix} a_1 + 2a_2\cos q_2 & a_3 + a_2\cos q_2 \\ a_3 + a_2\cos q_2 & a_3 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad C = \begin{pmatrix} -h\dot q_2 & -h(\dot q_1 + \dot q_2) \\ h\dot q_1 & 0\end{pmatrix},$$ $$\dot M - 2C = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & h(2\dot q_1 + \dot q_2) \\ -h(2\dot q_1 + \dot q_2) & 0\end{pmatrix}.$$

4. Why controllers care

PD control with gravity compensation is stable. Take \(\tau = g(q) - K_p\tilde q - K_d\dot q\) with \(\tilde q = q - q_d\) for a fixed goal and positive definite gains. Use the total energy of the closed loop as a Lyapunov function, \(V = \tfrac12\dot q^\top M\dot q + \tfrac12\tilde q^\top K_p\tilde q\). By the energy identity,

$$\dot V = \dot q^\top(\tau - g) + \dot q^\top K_p\tilde q = -\dot q^\top K_d\dot q \le 0 .$$

LaSalle's invariance principle then gives convergence to \(\tilde q = 0\): the only invariant set with \(\dot q = 0\) has \(K_p\tilde q = 0\). No Coriolis term appears in the proof, because skew-symmetry removed it.

Adaptive control. Slotine and Li (IJRR 1987) control an arm whose inertial parameters are unknown. The dynamics are linear in those parameters, \(M\ddot q_r + C\dot q_r + g = Y(q,\dot q,\dot q_r,\ddot q_r)\,\theta\), and the skew-symmetry of \(\dot M - 2C\) is what cancels the cross terms in their Lyapunov function, giving a globally convergent tracking controller with an update law \(\dot{\hat\theta} = -\Gamma\,Y^\top s\).

Passivity. The identity says the map from torque to joint velocity is passive with storage \(T + P\). That is the starting point for the passivity arguments in my note on passivity.

5. A check for learned dynamics

Learned models that predict \(M\) and \(C\) separately, such as Lagrangian networks (Lutter, Ritter and Peters; see the journal version, arXiv 2110.01894), can be checked for free: along any trajectory, \(\dot q^\top(\dot M - 2C)\dot q\) must vanish. A model that violates it creates or destroys energy, and a controller built on it inherits that error. Building the model from an energy, as Lagrangian networks do, makes the property hold by construction.

6. What to take away

  • The velocity terms shuffle energy between joints; their net work, counted with the changing inertia, is zero.
  • That one identity is why energy-based Lyapunov proofs for arms are short, and why adaptive control of manipulators works.
  • It is also a cheap physical consistency test for any learned dynamics model.
